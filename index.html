Why Matchnight

A management game that remembers

Quick to pick up, and the longer you stay, the more your club's story grows.

Your results write the story

Every season leaves its mark. The people around your club remember what you did, and each season ends with a chapter of your story. Club records name their holders and change hands, a retiring legend gets a guard of honour in his last home game, and academy graduates feel the pull of home.

Quick to play, real choices

A few clear calls each week, and every one counts: who plays, who you sign, what you spend. Follow the match on a 2D pitch or as text, or take the quick result and move on.

A whole football world

128 nations play on around you, with leagues abroad, cups, summer tournaments and award nights. Every player, club and town has a reputation that grows with what they do, and a tap on any name opens its card.

Built to last

Your career survives every update

It saves itself every match week, old saves keep working through every new version, and you have three slots plus save to a file.

Checked before it reaches you

Dozens of automated checks on phone and desktop before every version, old saves included, and long careers played out before each new build.

How you play

Your club, your calls

Eight jobs, one club. Each comes down to a few choices that matter, so the seasons move quickly.

Pick the team

Choose the formation and the approach, then the penalty taker and the captain. A gel bar shows how well your side plays together, and new signings need time to settle in.

Match night

Follow every move on a 2D pitch, with goals replayed up close, a commentary line and a crowd that reacts to every chance. Or read it as text, or take the quick result.

Run the backroom

Hire an assistant, a coach, a physio and a scout, each graded and with one clear job, and choose which jobs your assistant takes off your hands. When the matches pile up over Christmas, rotate the squad.

Do the deals

Offer a fee to the club and a wage to the player. Players weigh up your club's reputation, little-known gems come cheaper, and every refusal tells you why.

Balance the books

Wages, gate money, the club shop and the bar, plus kit deals, naming rights and season tickets. A well-run club can pay its way at every level.

Keep the board on side

Every board has its own personality and its own targets. A takeover can turn your club upside down, and when you go after a new job, the chair interviews you first.

Face the media

Big moments that need your call, weekly front pages from reporters you get to know, and a short why after every match. Then a Saturday night highlights show, and live cup draws with the pundits.

Build a legacy

Records that change hands, club legends, a Hall of Fame and farewells for the greats. Every trophy you win gets its own engraved plate in your trophy case.

How it's made

One fan, with AI tools

Matchnight is made by one football fan, who designs the game and plays every build. The code, the art and the game's text are written with Claude, an AI from Anthropic. The sound, the music and the commentator's voice are made with ElevenLabs, and the pictures of the big moments (in the highlights show and the season's comic pages) and the Story Mode poster with Canva's AI. No AI runs inside the game itself: everything you see and hear is made, played and checked before it reaches you.

The look

Crests and trophies

Every club has a crest in its own colours, with an emblem that fits its town. Every trophy gets a plate of its own: gold, silver or bronze at home by level, diamond for the European and world club cups and platinum for the manager awards.

Questions

Before you ask

When can I play it?

The first playable demo is expected in Q2 2027. Join the waiting list to play early, or follow @matchnightgame on Instagram to hear first.

Which platforms?

PC on Steam and Android first, with iOS and consoles after that.

Are the clubs and players real?

No. Every club is set in a real English town, but its name, players, ground and crest are invented. Nothing is licensed.

Is it made with AI?

Yes, and we say so openly: the code, art and text are made with Claude, the sound and music with ElevenLabs, the pictures of the big moments and the Story Mode poster with Canva's AI. No AI runs inside the game. More under How it's made.

News

How it's going

26 October 2026

Every match on the pitch

Matches now play out on a 2D pitch. Every chance is acted out the way it happened, from through balls and crosses to counter-attacks and corners, and finished with a header, a volley or a tap-in while the keeper dives full stretch. The players run, jump for headers and celebrate, goals are replayed up close, and the pitch wears as the season goes on. The latest build made it all more natural: players run at real speeds, a tackle comes from someone close by, and a match at normal speed takes about seven and a half minutes. In a hurry? Fast, Highlights and the text view are still there.

18 October 2026

Every club has a memory now

The last three builds gave Matchnight a long memory. Club records now name their holders and change hands when someone beats them, the greatest careers make the Hall of Fame, and a retiring legend gets a guard of honour in his last home game. Everything has a reputation too, from players to towns, so a famous name costs more and a hidden gem costs less. And every match now has a commentary line, with a text view if you'd rather read the match than watch it.

15 October 2026

The floodlights come on

Matchnight has found its voice. A floodlit intro switches the stadium on before the title screen, music plays on the start screens, and every match has its own sounds, with a crowd that reacts to every chance and a commentator for the big moments. You can also tap any player, club or score to open its card, with full career histories, match reports and a search that finds anyone as you type.

0.0.66.1A more natural pitch26 October 20268 changed · 1 fixed

Changed

  • Players move like footballers: they speed up and slow down, jog back into shape, and sprint only when a chance needs it.
  • Tackles and fouls happen up close: the player fouled has the ball, and a quick free kick is taken where he went down.
  • No more spinning on the spot, and keepers keep their eyes on the ball.
  • The ball is in proportion to the players, with a soft shadow and no line under it.
  • The match clock's seconds run smoothly; each half and extra time start on the minute, and the clock stops for a penalty shoot-out.
  • A match at Normal speed takes about 7 minutes 30 seconds; Fast and Highlights are as quick as before.
  • The commentary says more of what happens on the pitch: a player taking the ball on, quick free kicks and throw-ins, and a corner announced as it is taken when there was no time before.
  • The trophies you have won are now "Your trophy case", and England's cup history lists the winners, like the other trophies.

Fixed

  • "No camp" in the pre-season can be chosen when your club is in the red, and camps you can't pay for are greyed out.
0.0.66.0The 2D match view25 October 20267 added · 3 changed · 2 fixed

Added

  • Every chance is now acted out on the pitch the way it happened: through balls, crosses, cut-backs, one-twos, solo runs, counter-attacks, corners and long shots, finished with a header, a volley, a chip or a tap-in, and held, tipped over, blocked or off the post. Your players and your tactics shape how chances come about.
  • Players with arms and legs: they run, kick, jump for headers, dive full stretch and celebrate, each in their own style.
  • Goals are replayed from a closer camera at Normal speed (tap to skip).
  • A Near/Far camera button, a match clock with seconds, and a pitch that wears as the season goes on.
  • The Saturday show opens with the floodlights coming on, row by row, to its own theme music, and its replays show each goal as it was scored.
  • Three versions of every show picture, chosen to fit each goal; a saved penalty or a red card that decides a match now gets its own picture.
  • Your manager's office at the top of your club messages: from the site cabin in the lower leagues to the manager's suite in the Premier Division.

Changed

  • A match at Normal speed takes about 5 minutes 45 seconds; Fast and Highlights are as quick as before.
  • The commentary says how each chance ends and describes the new moves.
  • In the text view, quiet spells pass more quickly.

Fixed

  • A shot that was parried or blocked into a rebound now gets its ending in the commentary.
  • "Corner to..." is said again before every corner.

0.0.65.0The Boardroom25 October 202610 added · 5 changed · 2 fixed

Added

  • Your board has a mood, patient, ambitious or thrifty, from the club's owner, and sets three targets every season: a league place, a cup run and the money. A new Board page shows the chair, the owner, the mood, how confident they are and why it moved, and how each target stands.
  • Takeovers: now and then someone bids for your club and the board asks what you think. Back the new owners for their money and a demand with a deadline, oppose them, or leave it to the board. Other clubs change hands in the news.
  • Job interviews: every job offer starts with the chair's three questions. Answer in the spirit of their board and the job is yours, with your first target and some money to spend; answer badly and it goes to someone else.
  • Jobs open: when a club sacks its manager in mid-season, apply for the job, and after their next match they pick you or the man waiting.
  • Counter a bid for your player: name your price, watch the buyer come up round by round, and don't push their patience too far.
  • The career book: your whole career as one book that follows you from club to club, with a farewell chapter each time you leave.
  • The season in comic pages: at the end of each season its story plays as comic pages to swipe through, with the season's best players and the final table.
  • Every player now has a comic face, in the same look as Story Mode, wearing his club's shirt.
  • Three new buildings: a medical centre, a sports science lab and a scouting network. The richest rivals build too.
  • The Saturday show's studio with its three presenters, and each pundit's portrait beside their words.

Changed

  • Louder papers: the Daily Whistle shouts like a tabloid and the town paper is proud and one-eyed, with headlines in capitals.
  • Deadline day gets busier, with a round-up of every late deal.
  • The club's lists say whether a player who left has retired or where he plays now, and retired players who mattered keep a card.
  • The world club cups history is a table by year, like Europe's.
  • Match reports: "Key moments", "What clicked" and "What misfired".

Fixed

  • The Skip button on the show and the cup draw is readable in the light look.
  • A Saturday show you have already watched keeps its words when a Monday match joins its weekend.
